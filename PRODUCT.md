# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Primary**: Ambitious individuals (25-45) navigating African income realities — irregular earnings, multi-currency exposure, limited traditional banking options. They are freelancers, entrepreneurs, and professionals who want financial intelligence, not just another budgeting app.

**Secondary**: SME owners (3-50 employees) managing cash flow, vendor payments, and tax obligations. They need visibility into money movement without enterprise-software complexity.

**Context of use**: First visit to the public homepage. They arrive curious, skeptical, or actively searching for a better financial tool. They are comparing against traditional banks, budgeting apps, and wealth management platforms. The homepage must convert curiosity into a waitlist signup.

## Product Purpose

FINOVAULT is a financial intelligence platform — NOT a bank. It gives users the ability to see, understand, and control their money across accounts, currencies, and income patterns. The platform provides pattern recognition, actionable insights, and goal tracking built for the realities of African finance.

The public homepage exists to:
1. Establish brand identity and positioning against incumbents
2. Communicate the product vision before launch
3. Convert visitors into early-access waitlist members
4. Set clear expectations (product is in development, pricing is proposed)

## Brand Personality

**Three words**: Precise, Uncompromising, Calm authority.

**Voice**: Confident without arrogance. Technical without jargon. Direct without aggression. The brand speaks like a trusted financial advisor who respects your intelligence — never condescending, never salesy, never warm-for-the-sake-of-warm.

**Emotional goals**:
- Competence: "This team knows what they're building"
- Control: "I can finally see and understand my money"
- Intelligence: "This is a smarter way to think about finance"
- Aspiration: "I want to be the person who uses this"
- Security: "My money and data are safe here"

**Tone calibration**:
- NEVER: playful, friendly, warm, casual, corporate, generic, condescending
- ALWAYS: precise, direct, authoritative, restrained, premium

## Anti-references

**Visual anti-patterns to avoid**:
- Corporate fintech blues-and-curves (N26, Revolut marketing)
- Friendly-illustration SaaS (Mint,YNAB marketing)
- Generic SaaS landing page with gradient CTAs and stock photos of smiling people
- Dark mode "hacker aesthetic" with neon accents
- Glassmorphism and blurred backgrounds used decoratively
- Rounded-everything "approachable" design language
- Template-like identical card grids with icon + heading + text
- Small uppercase tracked eyebrows repeated above every section
- Numbered section markers (01 / 02 / 3) as default scaffolding

**Product anti-patterns to avoid**:
- "Bank-level security" claims
- Fake testimonials or endorsements
- Unverified traction metrics
- Rainbow gradients or glassmorphism as decoration
- Overly complex navigation

**Copy anti-patterns to avoid**:
- "We're on a mission to democratize..."
- "Join thousands of satisfied users"
- "革命ary" / "game-changing" / "disrupting"
- Any copy that doesn't say something specific

## Design Principles

1. **Luxury brutalism over corporate fintech** — High contrast, sharp edges, large editorial typography. NOT rounded, NOT friendly, NOT warm. The design communicates precision and seriousness.

2. **Content-first, decoration-last** — Every element exists because it communicates something. No decorative gradients, no stock photos for the sake of filling space, no animations that don't serve comprehension.

3. **Show, don't tell** — Demonstrate the product's intelligence through the homepage's own design. If we claim pattern recognition, show pattern recognition. If we claim financial intelligence, the page itself should feel intelligent.

4. **Restraint as a brand signal** — Limited colour palette, deliberate typography, purposeful whitespace. What we leave out matters as much as what we include. The purple accent appears sparingly. Gold appears for premium moments only.

5. **Accessible by default** — WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, prefers-reduced-motion support. Accessibility is not an afterthought; it is a design constraint that makes the product better.

6. **Honesty in presentation** — All statistics labelled "ILLUSTRATIVE". Pricing labelled "PROPOSED". Future features labelled "PLANNED" or "FUTURE". No fake urgency, no misleading claims. Trust is built through transparency.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** compliance minimum
- **Contrast ratios**: Body text ≥4.5:1 against background; large text ≥3:1
- **Keyboard navigation**: All interactive elements focusable and operable via keyboard
- **Screen readers**: Semantic landmarks, proper heading hierarchy, ARIA labels where needed
- **Reduced motion**: All animations respect `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`
- **Focus management**: Visible focus indicators, logical tab order
- **Responsive**: Mobile-first design, readable at all viewport sizes
- **Zoom**: Page remains functional at 200% zoom (no `user-scalable=no`)
